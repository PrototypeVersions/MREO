AI & Technology

Use technology to reduce coordination friction without giving software unlimited authority.

01 · Current Architecture

Web2 First

MREO can begin as a conventional web application using familiar databases, APIs, user accounts, permissions, documents, and transaction workflows.

02 · AI Assistance

Authorized AI Assistance

AI can prepare summaries, compare records, identify missing information, monitor deadlines, organize property activity, and initiate an approved workflow when the system gives it limited authority to do so.

03 · Future Architecture

Verification & Portability

Later implementations could add digital signatures, cryptographic hashes, portable property records, structured machine-readable data, and other Web3 or Web4 capabilities where those technologies provide a useful function.

AI Tasks

01
Prepare Summarize property records, transaction status, reconstruction activity, or portfolio information.
02
Compare Compare bids, documents, estimates, deadlines, milestones, or changing property information.
03
Monitor Identify missing records, approaching deadlines, stale verification, or other workflow exceptions.
04
Initiate Begin an authorized next step, such as requesting updated information or routing an item for human review.

Authority

AI should not receive unlimited control.

An AI agent should receive only the data, permissions, and authority required for its assigned task. Material legal, financial, contractual, settlement, ownership, or payment decisions can remain subject to the appropriate human or institutional approval.

Example Controls

Offer Analysis AI compares terms; seller selects
Reconstruction AI tracks milestones; authorized party approves payment
Settlement AI monitors requirements; regulated participants control funds
Portfolio Alerts AI identifies exception; responsible participant acts

Programmable Settlement

Automate instructions, not blind control of funds.

MREO can track whether settlement conditions have been satisfied and prepare authorized instructions. Where law or transaction structure requires regulated institutions to hold or transfer funds, those institutions remain responsible for the actual custody and release of money.

Web3 or Web4 technology is not required for the initial MREO application. Future versions could use cryptographic proofs, portable identities, machine-readable property records, or decentralized infrastructure where those tools improve verification, interoperability, or portability.
The technology should follow the operating problem rather than the reverse: first establish a useful property workflow, then add more advanced infrastructure where it solves a specific coordination or verification problem.

This prototype does not currently operate autonomous AI agents, smart-contract settlement, blockchain infrastructure, digital signatures, cryptographic property passports, or production transaction automation. These are proposed capabilities for later stages of the MREO architecture.
